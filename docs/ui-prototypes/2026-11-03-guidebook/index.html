Guidebook, carried through every tab

This round takes the Guidebook direction from one fusion page to the whole SMT1 game. All eight pages are linked to each other, so you can move around them like you would in the app. Every number comes from upstream's SMT1 data: all 252 demons, 105 skills, the race chart and 39 special recipes.

New in this round: a thumb index takes the place of the section nav, with one tab per section sticking out past the page edge. Moving between sections turns the page. The press run, where the sheet lands on the desk and the blue ink pulls into line, is now smoother and plays only on the first page you open. Click or press a key to skip it.

The pages

Open any of them, then move around with the thumb index on the right edge. Demon names and skill names link through to their own entries.

The system

Two inks on a paper sheet, on a dark desk. The app stays dark-only. The sheet is a paper context that flips the tokens, like Calling Card's paper, and the desk is the skin's dark surface.

Black ink carries text and data. Spot blue marks only the second plate: section bars, katakana that matters, the result and the current tab. Weak resistances print solid black, and null, repel and drain print in blue, so colour is never the only signal.

Components

  • Thumb index: section tabs on the page edge. At rest only the Japanese shows, hovering slides a tab out to reveal its English name, and the current tab is spot blue.
  • Running head: the section in Japanese, the title in Mincho, and the section's chapters (Calculator, Fusion chart, Special fusions).
  • Data box: a blue bar, the katakana written down the side, then the data. Used for parents, results, the demon entry, backup and settings.
  • Point box: a rounded blue frame with a halftone fill and a round mark (特殊, 注意), for special recipes and caveats.
  • Dense table: a double top rule, halftone on every other row, and group rows for each race or element.
  • Chips, segmented buttons, ink buttons: the pressed state is solid ink, and pressing a button sinks it 2 px into the paper.
  • Checklist tick: a square the reader marks, with a blue pen stroke that draws itself.

Voice

  • Titles get the guide's Japanese section name above an English title: 悪魔合体 Demon fusion, 合体早見表 Fusion chart.
  • Buttons, labels and errors stay plain: "Add to compendium", "Untick all 2", "No demon matches that filter."
  • Every page ends with a short source line saying where its numbers come from.

Porting it

What maps where

  • Tokens: spot blue becomes a new spot ramp in APP_PRIMITIVES. The desk is the skin's surface ramp in theme/skins.ts, and the paper context flips the tokens inside the sheet.
  • Skin: a new guidebook GameSkin, with its partial, lazy bundle entry and angular.json entry, the same as Abyss.
  • Thumb index: the shell's section nav, restyled. Chapters are the existing SectionLayout sub-tabs.
  • Page turn: Angular's withViewTransitions() router feature, with the CSS from the prototype.

What it costs

  • Fonts: the two Japanese families need subsets for the kana and kanji that are actually used: 252 demon names, 105 skill names and the section titles. This is the largest cost.
  • Data: the importer has to keep the katakana from jap-demons.json and jap-skills.json.
  • New views: the staircase chart, the checklist compendium and the "how to make it" list on each entry.

What works: the thumb index and chapter links, the page turn, the press run, every demon entry (from any name link), skill links that jump to and mark their row, search and filters on every list, picking squares on the fusion chart, the compendium ticks (kept in this browser only), the calculator's empty states and the inline confirms. Not wired: any fusion other than Barong + Rangda, triple and reverse fusion, export, import, backup, restore and reset.

Assumptions to confirm: upstream's SMT1 data is labelled SNES, and I haven't checked whether it matches the PS1 release. The compendium treats an SMT1 demon as having a fixed level and skills, so an entry is only a tick.
